# Reasoning moves

Choose a move that could change the decision. A useful recurring progression runs from a concrete inventory through framing, commonality, targeted change, simplification, and maintainability; it is not a required sequence.

- **Reframe:** Separate the outcome from its proposed implementation. “What required outcome cannot the current design provide?” Avoid reopening a requirement already established by evidence.
- **Compare mechanisms:** Contrast inputs, semantics, transformations, ownership, failure modes, and lifecycle. Shared orchestration does not imply identical business rules or data layers.
- **Locate control:** Trace a behavior to the priority, policy, parameter, or authority that determines it. Prefer an explicit, owned decision over repeated hidden conditionals; assess validation and change history too.
- **Verify relationships:** Pick a consequential value, reference, or decision. Locate its authoritative definition, then follow the relevant mapping through implementation and observed behavior to consumers. Check whether references resolve to real entities and whether meanings remain consistent; an explicit mapping can be correct even when names differ. Trace both origin and downstream impact, especially when definitions change. Distinguish intended state, inspected implementation, and runtime evidence; do not claim a live check from code alone. If evidence is absent, identify the unverified link and ask for the most useful artifact.
- **Trace recurrence:** Follow a failure from symptom through mechanism to the condition that permits it. Search adjacent paths sharing that condition. A hypothesis needs a discriminating check before being called a cause.
- **Expose invariants:** Turn a consequential implicit expectation into a scoped condition that could be violated. Establish why correctness requires it, what changes could break it, and the simplest boundary that can prevent or detect a violation. For recurring incidents, distinguish repairing the affected item from closing the gap that allowed the violation; check other paths sharing that gap. Treat a candidate condition as an assumption until justified, not as a newly discovered universal rule.
- **Subtract:** Remove concepts, services, layers, and exceptions one at a time mentally. Identify the required behavior each contributes. Retain complexity that buys a necessary property.
- **Compare proportionately:** Consider the current solution, a targeted change, and a broader mechanism only when relevant. Weigh migration, reversibility, operational effort, and ownership alongside functional fit.

Evidence sufficient to converge depends on the stakes. An experiment may resolve an uncertain performance claim; a clear responsibility boundary may settle a small design choice. Name unresolved evidence and its decision impact. Do not demand exhaustive certainty.

For relationship integrity, prefer the simplest reliable check at the relevant boundary: an existing constraint, schema or type check, contract test, referential check, static analysis, or CI/deployment validation. Existing catalog or lineage capabilities may help when already available. Ask what catches a stale reference or changed meaning and who maintains that check. Do not introduce centralized governance, a metadata framework, or continuous monitoring unless the risk and scale justify it. Stop once important links are supported and remaining uncertainty is proportionate.
